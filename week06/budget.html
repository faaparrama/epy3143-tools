One concept, four lines, and a number

Completeness

Before you start

A four-line card for one concept you teach: what's hard, what to cut, what the learner does, and a number. Wrong is fine here. Vague is not.

Only Part 6 accepts pasted text. Everything else is your own reasoning and has to be typed. If you drafted somewhere else for an access reason, tell Dr. Parra-Martinez and he will take it that way.

Before you write a line

Answer these three before you draft the card.

Use your M1 population, or one Starkville student.

Name it specifically — the words, the procedure, or the prior concept it depends on.

Pick one: retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, self-explanation, or dual coding.

Part 1 — Intrinsic

Readiness Check

What makes this hard in itself?

"Simplify" is not an answer — say what you cut.

Part 2 — Extraneous

Readiness Check

What will you strip out — including the tool's output?

Is it clutter on the learner's desk, or clutter for your own convenience?

Part 3 — Germane

Readiness Check

What will the learner do with it?

A picture on a slide is extraneous unless they build or read a structure with it.

Part 4 — The output limit

Readiness Check

Give a number — steps, items, or words per turn — and say why.

"Three steps because there are three steps" is not a reason. A reason names the learner.

Part 5 — Swap and audit

Readiness Check

Trade cards. Three questions only, then revise your own.

Could a tool follow this? Whose desk is the extraneous line about? Would the limit survive "just explain the whole thing"?

Name the line. "Nothing" needs a reason.

Part 6 — The mnemonic test

Not graded — feeds Reflection 4.

Prompt 1: Make me a mnemonic for [your concept's parts]. Prompt 2, fresh chat: What would a learner have to already understand for a mnemonic for [your concept] to be useful rather than a substitute for understanding? List it; do not make the mnemonic.

Some concepts need the list held first. Say which yours is.
