Three Kinds of Load

One concept you teach, its intrinsic, extraneous and germane load, and a limit for your tool

✓ Saved

Completeness

Before you start

Pick one concept you teach. Say what makes it hard by its nature, what adds load without adding learning, and what the learner will do that builds it. Then set a number: how much your tool (the tool you are building for the Final Project) may give a learner at once. Wrong is fine here. Vague is not.

Before you start

Answer these first.

The population you defined for your own Milestone.

Name it: a word, a procedure, or an earlier concept.

Pick one: retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, self-explanation, or dual coding. Say why in one sentence.

Part 1 — Intrinsic load

Readiness Check

Count the parts a learner must hold in mind at once. Lesson steps don't count: name what is hard about the idea itself.

Part 2 — Extraneous load

Readiness Check

Name the clutter you will cut. A learner's struggles and prior knowledge belong to the concept: they go in Part 1.

Part 3 — Germane load

Readiness Check

Name the verb: explain, compare, retrieve, draw. A picture on a slide counts only if they build or read a structure with it.

Part 4 — A limit for your tool

Readiness Check

Give a number: steps, items, or words per turn.

"Three steps because there are three steps" is not a reason. A reason names the learner.

Part 5 — Swap

Readiness Check

Trade with a partner. Read their Parts 1–4.

Is Part 1 about the idea, or about lesson steps? Is Part 2 clutter, or the learner's struggles? Would the limit survive "just explain the whole thing"?

Name the part. "Nothing" needs a reason.

Part 6 — The mnemonic test

Not graded. Feeds Reflection 4.

Prompt 1: Make me a mnemonic for [your concept's parts]. Prompt 2, fresh chat: What would a learner have to already understand for a mnemonic for [your concept] to be useful rather than a substitute for understanding? List it; do not make the mnemonic.

Some concepts need the list held first. Say which yours is.
