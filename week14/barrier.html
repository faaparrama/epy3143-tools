Lower the Barrier, Keep the Bar

One task from your subject, one learner, and a plan that changes the route and keeps the goal

Before you start

When a student struggles, most new teachers make the task easier. This page asks you to do the harder thing: find the barrier, remove it, and keep what the task is for. You start from a strength, check a UDL option for everyone, decide whether an AI tool gives access or does the learning, and write the access rule for your tool (the tool you are building for the Final Project). Supports Milestone 4.

Part 1 — The task, and what it is for

Readiness Check

Pick one real assignment from your subject. Then name the bar: what every student must learn or do by the end of it.

One assignment or activity. What students read, do and turn in.

If you can't name it, you can't protect it. This is the line Part 3 must keep.

Part 2 — A learner: strength first

Readiness Check

Choose a learner from the list, or a student you know (no names). Start with what they can do.

Learners you could choose

Tanyadyslexia with an IEP; real talent in math
Matthewkeeps the first of three spoken directions; stops midway, "what was I supposed to do next?"

A talent, a language, an interest, a way of thinking. Start here, as an asset-based teacher does.

Name the barrier in the task or the room: the print, the spoken directions, the language, the pace. Keep the student's ability out of it.

Part 3 — Two plans

Readiness Check

Write the plan most new teachers would write first. Be honest. Then write the one that keeps the bar.

Shorter, fewer, simpler, a video instead. Write it as you would have.

Challenge, opportunity, practice, self-efficacy, the expectation it sends. Accommodation or modification?

Same goal as Part 1. A new route. Say what stays the same and what changes.

Part 4 — One option for everyone

Readiness Check

UDL builds options in from the start. Add one option per principle to your task, for every student in the room. The goal from Part 1 stays the same.

A choice of topic, a link to students' lives, a challenge level they pick.

Audio with the text, a described diagram, a glossary in more than one language.

Speak it, build it, record it, draw it. The bar from Part 1 still has to show.

Part 5 — The AI check

Readiness Check

Name one tool in your plan: text-to-speech, speech-to-text, captions, a chatbot, an image describer. Then ask the week's question.

Text-to-speech on a history reading gives access. Text-to-speech on a decoding lesson offloads the learning. Which one is yours?

Part 6 — An access rule for your Final Project

Readiness Check

Milestone 4 is the accessibility pass: who your tool might still be leaving out. Write the rule your tool follows for one of them. Fill the three blanks.

Part 7 — Swap

Readiness Check

Read your partner's Parts 1, 3 and 6.

Check it against their Part 1. If the goal moved, the bar moved.
