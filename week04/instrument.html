Two items per trait. Do not over-read this.

The TIPI's authors built it for situations where brevity matters more than precision. A 50-item inventory would give you a more stable estimate of the same five things.

So treat these bars as an approximation of your personality traits. If a score surprises you, the most likely explanation is the instrument, not a hidden truth about you. The marker on each bar is the mean for Gosling and colleagues' US sample of 1,813 adults. However, do consider how these scores could reflect your learning behaviors. If a teacher knew your scores, what should they consider when teaching you?

Lower reappraisalYou do not often reframe a situation to change how you feel about it. Reappraisal is a learnable strategy rather than a trait — which matters, because it is one of the few socio-emotional skills a classroom can actually teach.
Moderate reappraisalYou reframe sometimes. Worth noticing when you do and when you don't — reappraisal tends to be available for other people's problems long before it is available for your own.
Higher reappraisalYou routinely change how you think about a situation to change how you feel. This is the strategy associated with better outcomes in the literature, and it is the one you can model out loud for students.
Lower suppressionYou tend to let emotion show. In a classroom this reads as authenticity, and it also means your students will know when you are frustrated — which is information you are giving them whether you mean to or not.
Moderate suppressionYou hold some things back. The question worth asking is which things, and from whom.
Higher suppressionYou often keep feelings from showing. Suppression works in the moment and costs more over time — it does not reduce the feeling, only the expression, and it tends to reduce connection with the people around you. Worth knowing before a year of managing thirty adolescents.

About the reference line on the ERQ.

Gross and John's sample was 1,483 US undergraduates. Suppression differed by gender — men averaged about 3.6, women about 3.1. The single marker is the average for the two groups.

A reference mean always averages over somebody. When you hand a screener to your own class, ask whose average it was built on, and whether the comparison is appropriate before drawing conclusions.

Notes

Your emotional stability, and your suppression. Lower stability with higher suppression is a combination that undermines emotional management: the feelings are strong and then are not expressed, which is effortful and leaves the people around you with no signal to respond to.

Reappraisal is what a good teacher scaffolds. Suppression is what a student does alone.

Remember Maddie from the opening case study? A student who is anxious, overthinks every assignment, and has started talking it through with an AI at two in the morning before she can begin.

AI may sound like it genuinely helps. It is patient, always available, and never sighs.

It will also generate a fourth and a fifth framing of the same worry, indefinitely, because it has no stake in her stopping. A teacher would have said "that's enough, start writing." The GenAI model cannot tell the difference between reflection and rumination, because telling that difference requires knowing her.
