Part 1 — The concept and the wrong idea

Readiness Check

A misunderstood schema or working model (not a missing fact) "Humans evolved from chimpanzees," not "they don't know the formula."

A misconception survives because it makes sense to the individual.

Part 2 — Three or four examples

Readiness Check

List examples, cases, or demonstrations that would move the misconception.

An example earns its place when it

SHOWSthe final product
EXPLAINSthe hidden process
PREDICTSthe likely errors
CONNECTSto the students' world
ALIGNSwith how it will be graded

Pick two: how can they change the misconception. "When I tell them ....." is an explanation, not an example.

Part 3 — The exemplar

Readiness Check

Pick the best one and say why it works.

Name the prediction the student makes and how it can fail.

Would this bore a student who already understands it? What would you do?

Part 4 — A rule for your Final Project

Readiness Check

M2 Part 4, in one sentence. Fill the four blanks.

Your misconception from Part 1.

Observable — something typed or answered, not "seems confused."

Your exemplar from Part 3.

The tool's default you're overriding — almost always "explains it correctly."
